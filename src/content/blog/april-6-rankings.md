OpenRouter crossed 5 million daily API calls this week. That's up from 3.6 million in January.

If you're not paying attention to API gateways, you're missing the biggest shift in how developers use models.

<!-- more -->

## Gateways are winning

A year ago, most developers went directly to OpenAI or Anthropic. Today, the gateway model — one API, many models — is becoming the default.

Why? Three reasons:

1. **No vendor lock-in.** Switch models by changing one parameter, not your entire codebase.
2. **Price discovery.** Gateways surface cheaper providers automatically. You don't need to shop around.
3. **Fallback handling.** If one model is down or slow, the gateway routes to another. Free reliability.

OpenRouter isn't the only one — SiliconFlow and Groq both offer gateway-style access — but the 5M number suggests gateways collectively are now handling a significant chunk of all AI inference.

## What this means for model rankings

Gateway usage data is becoming the best public signal of model popularity. Not benchmarks. Not Twitter sentiment. Actual API calls, aggregated across thousands of developers.

When a model climbs the OpenRouter rankings, it means real people are building real things with it. That's more useful information than any benchmark score.

## This week's notable movers

- **Mistral Large** cracked the top 15. Code-related usage is driving most of the growth.
- **DeepSeek V4** held steady at #9. The model has found its audience and isn't losing them.
- **Gemini 2.0** usage dipped slightly. Could be noise. Could be the beginning of a trend. Worth watching.

## The counterargument

Gateway rankings aren't perfect. They skew toward developers (not enterprise), they overrepresent certain regions, and they miss models that are primarily accessed through first-party APIs.

But they're the best public data we have. And they're getting more representative every month as gateways grow.

The takeaway: if you want to know which models actually matter, watch the gateway numbers. Not the press releases.
