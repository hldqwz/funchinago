April was brutal for AI pricing. In a good way, if you're a buyer.

Average price per million tokens across the top 50: down 11% month over month. That's on top of a 22% drop in Q1. The trend line is unmistakable.

<!-- more -->

## Who's cutting and why

**SiliconFlow** started the latest round. They cut prices on 8 models in April alone, some by as much as 40%. The strategy is transparent: buy market share now, monetize later.

**Groq** matched within days. Groq's advantage is hardware — their custom chips give them unit economics that software-only platforms can't touch. They can cut and still make money.

**OpenRouter** didn't cut directly but added three new providers to their routing layer, which pushed effective prices down about 7% through competition alone.

## The models getting cheapest fastest

Entry-level models are approaching zero:
- Small open-source models: some now under $0.05/million tokens
- Mid-tier models (Mistral class): $0.15-$0.30 range, down from $0.30-$0.50
- Premium tier (Claude, GPT-4): holding firmer, but still down 5-8%

The biggest percentage drops are in the mid-tier. That's where the competitive pressure is highest.

## What this means for your bill

If you locked in a model and a provider six months ago, you're probably overpaying by 30-40%. Not because anyone raised prices on you — because everyone else lowered theirs and you didn't notice.

Go to the model page. Check the cross-platform price table. Spend 5 minutes. Save actual money.

## How low can this go

There's a floor. Inference costs real compute, and GPUs aren't free. But we haven't hit the floor yet. My estimate: another 25-35% decline across the top 50 by end of year before things stabilize.

The beneficiaries are builders. Every dollar you save on inference is a dollar you can spend on better features, more experiments, or — imagine — profit.
