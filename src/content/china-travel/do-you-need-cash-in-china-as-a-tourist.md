China is often described as nearly cashless, and that is directionally true for many daily situations. But nearly cashless is not the same as completely cash-free. For first-time visitors, a small cash backup can still remove a lot of stress.

## Quick Answer

Yes, many tourists should still carry some cash in China, even if they plan to use [Alipay](/china-travel/articles/how-to-use-alipay-in-china-foreigner/) or [WeChat Pay](/china-travel/articles/how-to-use-wechat-pay-in-china-foreigner/). Mobile payment is usually the smoothest option, but cash can still help when an app fails, a card is blocked, or a small merchant cannot process your preferred method.

## What Travelers Usually Expect

Many visitors arrive expecting one of two extremes:

- everything will require cash, or
- cash is completely useless.

In practice, the reality is in the middle.

For many day-to-day purchases, mobile payment is often the easiest route. But cash still helps in some very practical moments:

- your phone battery is low,
- your data connection is unstable,
- your bank blocks a foreign-card transaction,
- a small merchant has trouble with your linked card flow,
- you need a fast backup on arrival day.

If you have not already read it, start with the main [How to Pay in China as a Tourist](/china-travel/articles/how-to-pay-in-china-tourist/) hub and then use this cash guide as the backup layer.

## Where Cash Still Helps

Cash is often most useful in the following situations:

- small food stalls or simple local counters,
- fast arrival-day purchases,
- short taxi or convenience scenarios when your app is not ready,
- emergency fallback when both QR apps and cards become inconvenient.

This does not mean cash will always be preferred. It means cash still has practical value when the digital plan breaks.

## How Much Cash Usually Makes Sense?

There is no universal amount, but for many visitors, the right idea is modest backup cash, not a full trip budget in banknotes.

A practical mindset:

- carry enough for transport, a meal, and a small emergency,
- do not rely on cash as your only system,
- keep it paired with your [China Travel Checklist Before You Fly](/china-travel/articles/china-travel-checklist-before-you-fly/) and at least one app-based plan.

## Practical Backup Plan

The safest first-trip setup usually looks like this:

- [How to Pay in China as a Tourist](/china-travel/articles/how-to-pay-in-china-tourist/) as the main strategy,
- [Alipay](/china-travel/articles/how-to-use-alipay-in-china-foreigner/) as a likely primary app,
- [WeChat Pay](/china-travel/articles/how-to-use-wechat-pay-in-china-foreigner/) as a useful second option,
- one or two physical cards,
- a small amount of cash.

Cash is not the star of the system. It is the safety net.
