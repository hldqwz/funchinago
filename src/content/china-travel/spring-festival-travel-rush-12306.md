If you travel in China around Lunar New Year, the biggest variable is not the weather or your itinerary. It is the fact that hundreds of millions of people are trying to move at the same time. Called chunyun, the Spring Festival travel rush is often described as the largest annual migration on Earth, and it rewards people who understand the ticket rules.

## Quick Answer

The 2026 railway chunyun ran **2 February to 13 March — 40 days**. The public holiday was **15 to 23 February**, with Chinese New Year itself on 17 February (Year of the Horse). China Railway forecast about **539 million rail passenger trips**, up 5.0% year on year.

Train tickets go on sale **15 days before the travel date**. That window is the whole game: for the first day of the rush, tickets went on sale on 19 January 2026; for the peak outbound day, 13 February, they went on sale on 30 January; for the peak return day, 23 February, on 9 February. The railway's own reporting puts the busiest ticket-sale periods at 29 January–1 February and 7–10 February.

If you remember one practical rule, make it this: decide your route before the window opens, have your passport details ready in the app, and buy both outbound and return tickets as soon as your date becomes available.

## What 15 days actually means

China Railway sells tickets on a rolling 15-day pre-sale period, not a fixed seasonal release. So "15 days before" moves with your travel date. For a trip on 2 March, for example, the sale date would be around 15 February. You can work it out by counting back 15 days, but you should confirm with the 12306 app, because the sale start time also varies by station.

That last point catches people out. Different stations release tickets at different times of day — in Beijing during the 2026 rush, major stations had sale starts spread across 8:00, 10:00, 12:00, 12:45 and 16:00. In the 12306 app you can check a station's sale time under the "my" / sale-time query function, and you can subscribe to a sale reminder for a chosen date and departure station. Use it. Popular trains on peak days can sell out within minutes.

Three features are worth preparing before the window opens:

- **Pre-fill passenger details.** Save your name and passport information as a passenger in the app so you can submit an order in one or two taps.
- **Use the waitlist (候补).** When tickets sell out, the official waitlist is the legitimate route. China Railway said new capacity created for the rush would be allocated first to waitlisted passengers.
- **Check the exact name on your passport.** A mismatch between your booking and your travel document can block entry at the gate.

There was also a new passenger right in 2026: if you bought a ticket by mistake on 12306 for travel on or after 2 February, you could cancel it free of charge within 30 minutes of payment, provided the train was still more than four hours away. It is a narrow rule, but useful to know.

## The city contrast that surprises visitors

The migration creates a strange split that regular visitors learn to expect. The big migrant cities — Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou — feel noticeably quieter in the days around New Year's Eve. Commuters have left, metro cars empty out, and roads that are usually jammed become pleasant for walking. Meanwhile, famous domestic holiday destinations and temple fairs swing the other way: hotels in places like Sanya, Lijiang or Harbin can cost far more, and popular sites need bookings well ahead.

Restaurants follow their own calendar. Small family-run shops commonly close for the first few days of the new year while owners celebrate with family, then reopen gradually. Hotel restaurants, mall food floors, chain outlets and convenience stores generally keep trading. If you are in a residential neighbourhood, do not assume your mapped noodle shop will be open; check, or keep a backup within walking distance. These are widely reported traveller patterns rather than official statistics, so treat them as planning heuristics.

## Practical advice for foreign visitors

- **Avoid intercity travel on peak days if you can.** The pre-festival peak was 13 February in 2026 and the return peak 23 February. Stay in one city across the holiday and explore locally instead.
- **Book outbound and return together.** A cheap outbound ticket is not a bargain if you cannot get back.
- **Use official channels only.** Book through 12306 or the airline, not private resellers or social-media ticket offers.
- **Allow extra station time.** Stations are large, security queues grow, and passport gates add a step.
- **Check what is open on the day.** Major attractions and metro lines keep running, but individual sites may use reduced holiday hours. Reconfirm each morning.
- **Consider the reward.** Lantern displays, temple fairs and red decorations make this one of the most vivid weeks to be in China, and there are fewer foreign tourists than in summer.
