If you are going to Guangzhou in spring or autumn, check the Canton Fair calendar before you book anything else. It is the single biggest recurring disruption to the city's hotels and transport, and it catches many independent travellers by surprise.

## Quick Answer

The Canton Fair (China Import and Export Fair) runs twice a year in Guangzhou, and each session is split into three phases. For 2026 the organiser lists the spring session (139th fair) as **15–19 April, 23–27 April and 1–5 May**, and the autumn session (140th fair) as **15–19 October, 23–27 October and 31 October–4 November**. These are the **2026 dates — confirm on cantonfair.org.cn** before you travel, because the pattern repeats but exact windows can be adjusted.

During each phase, hotels across Guangzhou become expensive and hard to find, Metro Line 8 and roads around the Pazhou exhibition centre get crowded, and restaurants near the venue fill up at lunch with buyers. The fair runs through weekends too. If you are not attending, the simplest fix is to shift your dates by a few days: before mid-April or after 5 May, and before mid-October or after 4 November.

## What the fair actually is

The fair is not a small trade show. According to the organiser, it was founded in 1957, is co-hosted by China's Ministry of Commerce and the Guangdong provincial government, and is organised by the China Foreign Trade Centre. It is held at the China Import and Export Fair Complex at No. 382 Yuejiang Zhong Road in Haizhu District. The official overview gives an exhibition area of about 1.55 million square metres and roughly 32,000 exhibitors, with daily business hours of 9:30 to 18:00.

That scale matters for visitors. Tens of thousands of buyers arrive for each phase, and the organiser's own page lists about 314,000 overseas buyers from 220 countries and regions for the spring 2026 session. Treat that figure as the organiser's reported number rather than an independently audited statistic; the practical point is simply that the city absorbs a very large, time-concentrated influx.

The three phases cover different product categories: the first is heavy on electronics, machinery and vehicles, the second on home goods, gifts and furniture, and the third on textiles, clothing, food and medical products. If you are attending for business, the phase you choose determines almost everything about your trip.

## What the surge looks like on the ground

Buyer-focused travel sources consistently report the same pattern, even though there is no official citywide price index we can quote. Hotels adjacent to the Pazhou complex are often the first to sell out and the most expensive; rates in other districts also rise because the whole city tightens at once. Rooms that are easy to book in a normal week can be gone weeks ahead.

Transport feels it too. Line 8 stops at Pazhou, and the station is the main pedestrian funnel into the halls. The crush is worst in the morning, around the 9:30 opening, and again when a phase ends and thousands of people move on the same day. Taxis and ride-hailing cars near the venue become slow and expensive to find.

Lunch is the other squeeze. Restaurants inside and immediately around the fairground are routinely occupied by buyers taking business meetings, so a walk-in visitor can struggle to get a table at noon. This is a rhythm locals know well: eat early or late, or walk a few extra blocks away from the exhibition centre.

## How to plan around it

If you are attending the fair:

- **Register early.** Badge applications and pre-registration run through the official site and the Canton Fair app. Sort this before you fly.
- **Book accommodation as far ahead as you can.** Refundable rates cost a little more but protect you if your plans change. Last-minute rooms exist, but at fair-week prices.
- **Sleep away from Pazhou if your budget is finite.** Yuexiu and Liwan keep you close to the old city and the food; Tianhe has more business hotels and evening dining. All are workable by metro.
- **Avoid arriving or departing on a phase-change day.** The 19th/20th, 27th/28th and 5th/6th patterns concentrate departures and arrivals.
- **Use the metro rather than a taxi for the last mile.** If your hotel is within a few stops of Pazhou on Line 8, the commute is straightforward.

If you are just travelling and the fair overlaps your dates, shift the trip if you can. Guangzhou reward two or three unhurried days, and they are much easier outside the fair windows. If you cannot move, stay in Yuexiu or Liwan, plan your lunches away from Haizhu, and allow extra time for any trip that touches Line 8.

For rail connections, use the official [12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/index.html) rather than resellers. Our [Guangzhou first-time visitor guide](/china-travel/articles/guangzhou-travel-guide-first-time-visitors/) has district and metro notes that pair well with this page.
