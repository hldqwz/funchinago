Zhuhai sits on the west bank of the Pearl River estuary, directly across the border from Macau. It has no metro, no imperial history, and no skyline worth a pilgrimage — what it has is sea air, a 28-kilometre waterfront promenade, an opera house on its own island, and a theme park families plan whole trips around.

## Quick Answer

Spend three days in Zhuhai: one for the Lovers' Road coast, one for old Xiangzhou, one for Chimelong. Base yourself along the waterfront, move by bus, ride-share and shared bike, and add Macau if you have a fourth day. Budget ¥550–900 per person per day for a comfortable mid-range trip, with the Chimelong ticket on top.

## Why Zhuhai Belongs on a First China Trip

Most first-time itineraries stack Beijing, Shanghai, and Xi'an back to back — magnificent, but exhausting. Zhuhai offers a breather.

- **A real waterfront city.** Lovers' Road runs for kilometres along the sea, with beaches, boardwalks, lawns, and fishing spots stitched together. Joggers, evening dancers, and weekend families actually use it.
- **Landmarks with design merit.** The Zhuhai Opera House — two shell forms nicknamed the Sun Moon Shells (日月贝) — sits alone on Yeli Island. The Fisher Girl (珠海渔女), holding a pearl above the waves, is 8.7 metres of granite raised in 1982.
- **Chimelong Ocean Kingdom.** One of the largest ocean parks on earth: a record-breaking aquarium, whale sharks, penguins, parades, and a fireworks show people stay to the end for.
- **Macau is next door.** Gongbei Port puts you in Macau in under an hour. Cantonese seafood at lunch, Portuguese egg tarts by dinner.
- **Easy logistics.** Intercity trains link Zhuhai Station with Guangzhou South in about an hour for ¥70; the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge shuttle connects HKIA with Zhuhai in roughly 45 minutes.
- **Gentle prices.** Hotels, seafood, and transport cost less than in Guangzhou or Shenzhen, let alone Macau.

## Who Should Visit Zhuhai?

- **First-timers who want a soft landing.** Smaller scale, slower pace, and open sea air make a gentler introduction to China than Beijing.
- **Families.** Chimelong alone justifies the trip for kids, and the flat promenades are stroller-friendly.
- **Couples and photographers.** Lovers' Road at sunset, the opera house at blue hour, the harbours at dawn.
- **Macau and Hong Kong extenders.** If either is on your route, Zhuhai turns a border hop into a three-day coastal stay.
- **Repeat visitors.** Been to the big five cities and want the China that Chinese tourists love? This is it.

Zhuhai is less ideal if this is your only China trip and you want the greatest-hits list. Treat it as a complement to the classics, not a replacement. Not sure how it fits? Run the [China City Picker](/tools/china-city-picker/) before you lock your route.

## How Many Days Do You Need in Zhuhai?

- **Two days** covers the essentials at a brisk pace: one day on the Lovers' Road coast (Fisher Girl, beaches, opera house at night), one day for Xiangzhou old town or Chimelong.
- **Three days is the sweet spot.** Coast, then Xiangzhou's parks and old streets, then Chimelong. No backtracking — this is the itinerary below.
- **Four or more days** adds a Macau day trip through Gongbei Port, slower seafood dinners, or a ferry to the outer islands.

## Best Time to Visit Zhuhai

Zhuhai has two good windows: late October to mid-December, and mid-March to late April.

- **Late October to mid-December is the best of the year.** Roughly 22–28°C in late October, 18–25°C in November, 14–21°C by December. Humidity drops, skies clear, typhoon season ends. National Day week (1–7 October) is the exception: book Chimelong-area hotels well ahead.
- **Mid-March to late April is the second window.** Around 15–25°C, kapok and yellow trumpet trees in flower, the humid "returning south" spell finished, seas calm enough for island ferries.
- **May to September is hot, wet and typhoon-prone.** Expect 26–32°C, heavy afternoon showers, and a real chance a typhoon shuts ferries, the bridge shuttle or Chimelong for a day. Keep one flexible day and check the marine forecast each morning.
- **January and February are mild and quiet.** Around 13–19°C and dry, with thin crowds — until Chinese New Year week, when hotels fill and prices double.

## Best Things to Do in Zhuhai

### 1. Walk the Lovers' Road Coastal Strip

Lovers' Road (情侣路) is the promenade linking the Love Post Office, the Fisher Girl, Xianglu Bay Beach, City Balcony viewpoints, and the Yeli Island causeway. It is free, never closes, and is where the city spends its evenings. Walk it in sections, unlock a shared bike between stops, and go early morning for local life or late afternoon for the light.

### 2. See the Fisher Girl Statue

The Zhuhai Fisher Girl (珠海渔女) — a granite figure holding a pearl above the surf — is the city's symbol, and the setting does most of the work. There is no gate and no ticket; you walk up from the promenade. Twenty minutes, best in the soft light before sunset, once the tour buses have gone.

<img src="/china-travel/images/zhuhai-fisher-girl-statue.webp" alt="The Zhuhai Fisher Girl statue holding a pearl aloft on its rock in Xianglu Bay" width="1200" height="1800" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

### 3. Visit the Love Post Office

A white, lighthouse-style post office near the beach end of the promenade at 88 Qinglü Middle Road (情侣中路88号). Built for photos, with postcards carrying a special local postmark you can mail home. Free, no booking, open roughly 11:00–21:00 on weekdays and 10:00–22:00 at weekends.

### 4. Cross to Yeli Island and the Sun Moon Shells

Yeli Island (野狸岛) is reached by causeway and ringed by a shaded 3-kilometre cycling path. At its tip stands the Zhuhai Opera House (日月贝): twin shells rising from the water, lit from about 18:30 to 22:30. The island, plaza and exterior cost nothing; the building opens roughly 10:00–18:30 on days without a performance, and concerts are ticketed through the theatre's official WeChat account. Arrive at blue hour.

![The Sun Moon Shells of Zhuhai Opera House on the Yeli Island waterfront](/china-travel/images/zhuhai-opera-house-hero.jpg)

### 5. Climb Jingshan Park

Jingshan Park (景山公园) rises by the sea in Jida. Park entry is free; the cable car is the paid part — about ¥65 return, or ¥75 for cable car up and toboggan down. The slide is 630 metres of hand-braked track through the trees toward the water, and it is more fun than it has any right to be. Under-1.1-metre children and over-60s cannot ride it. The summit looks over Lovers' Road, the Fisher Girl, and on clear days the HZMB bridge. Go in the last two hours of daylight.

### 6. Wander Yuanming New Garden

Yuanming New Garden (圆明新园) is a full-scale homage to Beijing's destroyed Old Summer Palace, blending imperial layouts with Jiangnan landscaping and scheduled fountain shows. Daytime entry has been free and reservation-free on ordinary days since 2024; the evening session is ticketed from about ¥48. Rules here have changed more than once, so check first — and bring a hat, because shade is scarce at midday.

### 7. Slow Down in Beishan Old Courtyard

Beishan (北山大院) is a preserved Lingnan village of grey-brick lanes turned quietly creative: indie shops, small eateries, galleries, tea spots, no hard sell. The paving is uneven, so wear flat shoes and wander without a timetable.

### 8. Spend a Full Day at Chimelong Ocean Kingdom

On Hengqin Island, Chimelong Ocean Kingdom (长隆海洋王国) is the heavyweight: whale-shark halls, penguin and polar zones, stunt shows, parades, and a closing fireworks display worth staying for. Adult admission is ¥395 on standard days and ¥450 on designated peak days (weekends, public holidays, July–August); children 1.0–1.5 metres pay ¥280–315. Pricing is calendar-based, so confirm your date on Chimelong's official channels. Arrive at opening, book show times on entry, and plan a full day.

## A Realistic Three-Day Zhuhai Itinerary

Each day is one line with no backtracking, connected by short, cheap rides.

| Day | Route | How It Flows |
|-----|-------|--------------|
| **Day 1 — The coast** | Love Post Office → Fisher Girl → Xianglu Bay Beach → City Balcony → Yeli Island → Sun Moon Shells | Start mid-morning at the post office, walk or bike the promenade south through the beach stops, cross to Yeli Island in the afternoon, and finish at the opera house plaza for the evening lights. Rides between coastal stops cost around ¥15 or less. |
| **Day 2 — Old Xiangzhou** | Zhuhai Museum → Jingshan Park → Yuanming New Garden → Fuhuali → Beishan Old Courtyard → Xiawan Night Market | Museum first (cool and quiet), Jingshan before the heat peaks, the garden in the softer afternoon light, then Fuhuali and Beishan for the golden-hour wander, ending at Xiawan Night Market (夏湾夜市) for dinner grazing near Gongbei. |
| **Day 3 — Chimelong** | Chimelong Ocean Kingdom, full day | Take the whole day on Hengqin Island: aquarium halls in the morning, shows and parades midday, coasters and the finale fireworks in the evening. Nothing else scheduled — you will need the evening to recover. |

**Practical tips for this route:**

- **Sun discipline.** Coastal UV is strong even under cloud. Hat, sunscreen, water for Day 1; skip the open promenade from noon to 2 PM.
- **Timing beats crowds.** The Fisher Girl and Love Post Office are empty early and golden late. The opera house only earns its reputation after dark.
- **Footwear.** Day 1 is bikes and boardwalks; Day 2's Beishan lanes want flat, grippy soles on old stone.
- **Buses and bikes.** City buses cost ¥1 flat anywhere in the city. Shared bikes and e-bikes cover promenade hops — the app quotes the rate before you unlock.
- **Rain plan.** Swap Day 1's beaches for the Zhuhai Museum and Fuhuali's covered streets, and keep Chimelong (mostly indoors) as your weather-proof anchor.

If you only have two days, merge Day 1 and Day 2 by cutting the museum and Fuhuali. With four, add Macau through Gongbei Port — under an hour each way outside rush periods.

## What to Eat in Zhuhai

Zhuhai eats like a Cantonese fishing city that happens to sit next to Macau: seafood first, everything else second.

| Must-Try | What It Is | Where to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| Hengqin oysters (横琴蚝) | Plump local oysters — steamed with garlic, baked with cheese, or eaten raw at higher-end spots | Seafood streets on Hengqin Island and Gongbei |
| Fresh-caught seafood dinner | Tanks-to-table fish, shrimp, crab, and shellfish cooked Cantonese-light: steamed, blanched, or congee-based | Wanzai seafood street and old Xiangzhou eateries |
| Morning tea dim sum | Har gow, siu mai, cheung fun, and egg tarts in unhurried tea-house style | Traditional tea houses in Xiangzhou old town |
| Night-market grazing | Grilled skewers, oyster omelets, sugar-water desserts (糖水), fruit juices | Xiawan Night Market near Gongbei |
| Macanese crossovers | Egg tarts, pork-chop buns, and milk tea showing the Macau influence across the border | Bakeries and cha-chaan-teng-style cafes citywide |

Wanzai Seafood Street (湾仔海鲜街), by the ferry pier, is the classic experience: pick your fish from tanks, agree the price by weight, carry it upstairs and pay a cooking fee of roughly ¥25 per dish. That lands around ¥100–200 per person; a sit-down seafood restaurant runs ¥150–300, more if you order lobster. Agree the price before anything goes in the steamer.

Ordering without Chinese is doable: point at tanks and neighbouring tables, use a photo-translation app on wall menus, and learn "这个" (zhège — "this one"). For a deeper primer, read [What to Eat in China](/china-travel/articles/what-to-eat-in-china/) before you fly.

## Where to Stay in Zhuhai

- **Jida / Lovers' Road strip (吉大)** — The pick for Day 1: hotels along the coastal road put the Fisher Girl, the beaches, and the post office within walking distance.
- **Old Xiangzhou (香洲老城区)** — Near the railway station, Jingshan Park, and Beishan, with the strongest neighborhood eating. Best for food lovers arriving by intercity train.
- **Gongbei Port area (拱北口岸)** — Next to Fuhuali and Xiawan Night Market, liveliest after dark. Best for anyone crossing into Macau.
- **Hengqin / Chimelong area (横琴)** — Resort hotels around the park, some with direct park access. Best for families doing Chimelong — stay here on night two or three and skip the commute.

Book Chimelong-area hotels well ahead for weekends and holidays; elsewhere in the city, mid-range comfort at ¥300–500 a night is easy to find.

## What a Zhuhai Trip Actually Costs

Zhuhai's prices are the main reason to put it on a first China trip. Almost everything on the waterfront is free.

**Daily budget.** Budget travellers who take the bus, eat noodles and seafood at street level, and stay in a simple double spend around ¥280–420 a day. Mid-range travellers — sea-view room, sit-down seafood, the occasional taxi — spend ¥550–900. Add the Chimelong ticket on the day you go, and treat Macau as a separate budget: hotels and restaurants there run two to three times Zhuhai prices.

**What costs nothing.** Lovers' Road, Xianglu Bay Beach, the Fisher Girl, the Love Post Office, Yeli Island, the opera house plaza and exterior, Jingshan Park entry, Beishan's lanes, and the waterfront at Xiawan after dark.

**Check before you pay.** Chinese ticket prices are set per season and change without much notice. These figures were checked in October 2026 — confirm the current rate on the official channel or in the booking app on the day.

## Practical Tips: Macau Border, Transport, Payment and Language

- **Getting there.** Zhuhai Jinwan Airport (ZUH) runs an airport bus into the city for ¥25–30 (about 70 minutes to Gongbei); a taxi to Lovers' Road costs roughly ¥80–100. By rail, aim for Zhuhai Station (next to Gongbei) or Mingzhu Station (near Xiangzhou). From Hong Kong International Airport, the bridge shuttle reaches Zhuhai in about 45 minutes.
- **The Macau factor.** Gongbei is one of the world's busiest land crossings — details in the section below. Mobile payment and SIM setups that work on the mainland may behave differently across the border.
- **Getting around.** No metro — the city runs on buses (¥1 flat), ride-shares (¥10–12 flagfall, most urban hops under ¥30), and shared bikes along the coast. Skip the rental car: downtown parking is expensive and congested.
- **Payment.** Set up Alipay before arrival as your primary tool; WeChat Pay as backup. Details in [How to Pay in China as a Tourist](/china-travel/articles/how-to-pay-in-china-tourist/). Keep a little cash for older stalls and night-market vendors.
- **Language.** Mandarin works everywhere; Cantonese dominates casual conversation. English signage thins out fast — download offline translation and save your hotel name in Chinese characters. [Best Apps for Traveling in China](/china-travel/articles/best-apps-for-traveling-in-china/) covers the downloads.
- **Trains.** Book on the official 12306 English service — see [How to Book High-Speed Train Tickets](/china-travel/articles/how-to-book-high-speed-train-tickets-china-foreigner/).
- **Entry rules.** Check whether your nationality qualifies for [China's 240-hour visa-free transit](/china-travel/articles/china-240-hour-visa-free-transit/) or a visa-free arrangement before you go.
- **Prep list.** Run the [China Travel Checklist Generator](/tools/china-travel-checklist/) so payments, internet, and documents are sorted before you land.

## Crossing to Macau: Gongbei, Hengqin and the HZMB Port

Zhuhai has three usable land crossings into Macau, and picking the wrong one costs an hour.

- **Gongbei Port (拱北口岸).** Open 06:00–01:00. It is 500 metres from Zhuhai Station — follow the signs from exit B along the covered walkway. Off-peak the crossing takes 20–40 minutes; on a weekend afternoon or public holiday, budget 60–90 minutes. Weekday peaks are 07:00–09:00 and 17:00–19:00, and Golden Week or Chinese New Year is slow all day. You emerge at Macau's Border Gate, where buses, taxis and free casino shuttles fan out.
- **Hengqin Port (横琴口岸).** Open 24 hours, on the south side next to Chimelong. If you are staying on Hengqin or heading for Cotai and Taipa rather than the Macau peninsula, this is the smarter crossing — 10–15 minutes by taxi from the park and far quieter than Gongbei.
- **HZMB Zhuhai road port (港珠澳大桥珠海公路口岸).** Open 24 hours and built mainly for the bridge shuttle and coaches to Hong Kong. Use it for Hong Kong, not for a straight Macau day trip.

**Qingmao Port (青茂口岸)** also runs 24 hours and links to Zhuhai Station by covered walkway, but its joint one-stop channels are built around mainland, Hong Kong and Macau travel permits; most foreign passports are sent to Gongbei or Hengqin instead.

**On the 240-hour visa-free transit.** Gongbei has long been on the National Immigration Administration's list of eligible transit entry ports; Hengqin and the HZMB port were added on 5 November 2025. But it is a transit policy: you need an onward ticket to a third country or region, and a same-day return to Macau is not that. Treat the entry as single-use — leaving for Macau or Hong Kong does not top the clock up. Rules here change faster than guides track, so confirm your own case on the immigration hotline (12367) before booking.

**Macau runs its own immigration.** Mainland exit control and Macau entry control are two separate processes, and re-entering the mainland needs a valid entry basis — a visa, a residence permit, or a fresh visa-free entry. Most Western passports enter Macau visa-free, but check your own nationality's rule.

## Common Mistakes First-Time Visitors Make in Zhuhai

- **Treating it as a half-day stop.** Rushing the coast plus Chimelong into 36 hours is how people leave exhausted and underwhelmed.
- **Going to the opera house at noon.** The Sun Moon Shells are a daylight sculpture and a nighttime spectacle — midday gives you neither. Go late.
- **Underestimating the sun.** Coastal Zhuhai burns visitors who "don't feel hot" in the sea breeze.
- **Wearing the wrong shoes.** Island causeways, Beishan stone lanes, and a full Chimelong day punish heels and flimsy sandals.
- **Skipping the Macau paperwork check.** Assuming "it's right there" without verifying Macau entry rules for your passport has ruined more than one day trip.
- **Driving.** Renters usually spend the savings on parking and traffic; the bus-bike-ride-share triangle is faster.
- **Eating only in malls.** The best food is in Xiangzhou's old streets and at Wanzai and Xiawan. Follow the local crowds, as our [China travel mistakes guide](/china-travel/articles/china-travel-mistakes-first-trip/) keeps repeating.

## Zhuhai vs Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Macau

- **Zhuhai vs Guangzhou.** Guangzhou wins on food depth, history, and urban energy; Zhuhai wins on sea, air, and relaxation. Pair them: fly into Guangzhou, decompress in Zhuhai. See our [Guangzhou guide](/china-travel/articles/guangzhou-travel-guide-first-time-visitors/).
- **Zhuhai vs Shenzhen.** Shenzhen is a tech megacity with theme parks and shopping; Zhuhai is smaller, calmer, and has a beach culture Shenzhen mostly lacks. Business and gadgets: Shenzhen. Sea and slowness: Zhuhai.
- **Zhuhai vs Macau.** They share a border but almost nothing else: Macau is dense, Portuguese-flavored, casino-lit, and expensive; Zhuhai is spacious, green, activity-rich, and affordable. The classic move is sleeping in Zhuhai and day-tripping to Macau, where the same seafood dinner costs two to three times as much.

## What Visitors Often Miss About Zhuhai

- **The fishing-harbour mornings.** Before the promenade crowds wake, the small harbours unload the night's catch — gulls, dialect bargaining, diesel and salt air. Worth one early alarm.
- **The island ferry perspective.** Seeing the coastline, the opera house, and the bridge from the water reframes the city. Ask your hotel about current outer-island ferries.
- **Beishan after 9 PM.** The day crowds leave, the lanterns stay on, and the lanes turn atmospheric over a final bowl of sugar-water dessert.
- **The bridge itself.** The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge runs 55 kilometres, and the shuttle ride across open sea is half the experience of arriving.
- **Seasonal calendars.** Oyster season, lychee season, mango season — Zhuhai's markets rotate hard with the year, and eating with it is half the fun.

## Check Official Sources Before You Travel

- Confirm Chimelong Ocean Kingdom opening hours, show schedules, and ticket prices on Chimelong's official channels before your visit day — the park uses calendar-based pricing and schedules shift seasonally.
- Verify the current entry policy for Yuanming New Garden and Zhuhai Museum (reservations are common at major mainland attractions) rather than relying on older blog posts.
- Check typhoon and marine forecasts during summer travel; ferry and outdoor-show cancellations follow official warnings.
- Confirm Macau entry requirements for your nationality and the operating hours of the port you intend to use on your travel dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Zhuhai

### How much does Chimelong Ocean Kingdom cost?

Adult admission is ¥395 on standard days and ¥450 on designated peak days such as weekends, public holidays and July–August. Children between 1.0 and 1.5 metres pay ¥280–315, and under-1.0-metre children enter free with a paying adult. Prices follow a calendar, so check Chimelong's official channels for your date.

### Can I visit Macau for the day from Zhuhai?

Yes, and it is the main reason many travellers add Zhuhai to a route. Gongbei Port is open 06:00–01:00 and sits 500 metres from Zhuhai Station; Hengqin Port runs 24 hours and suits anyone staying near Chimelong. Macau sets its own entry rules, so check them separately from mainland China's.

### Do I need a visa to cross into Macau?

Macau issues its own entry permissions, independent of your mainland visa. Many Western, Japanese, Korean and Southeast Asian passports enter visa-free for 30 days or more; a handful of nationalities must apply in advance. Check the Macau SAR immigration pages for yours before you queue.

### Is Zhuhai easy to get around without a metro?

Yes. Buses charge a flat ¥1 on every route, ride-shares start at ¥10–12, and shared bikes cover the waterfront. The one gap is the airport: Jinwan is 40–60 minutes from downtown, served by a ¥25–30 bus or an ¥80–100 taxi.

### What is a realistic daily budget in Zhuhai?

Budget travellers spend around ¥280–420 a day and mid-range travellers ¥550–900, counting a room, three meals, local transport and one paid attraction. Chimelong day adds its ¥395–450 ticket. Zhuhai runs cheaper than Guangzhou or Shenzhen and far cheaper than Macau.

### When is the best time to visit Zhuhai?

Late October to mid-December is the steadiest stretch: 18–28°C, low humidity, no typhoon risk. Mid-March to late April is the second window, at roughly 15–25°C. Avoid July to September if you can — heat, rain and typhoons can close ferries and the theme park.

## Final Thoughts

Three days here is a specific kind of China trip: a harbour morning with the catch still moving, a bike path with the sea on one side, two white shells lighting up after dark, one long exhausting day among whale sharks. Nothing is a UNESCO site and nothing is trying to be. What you get is the coastal, Cantonese, small-city China the Beijing–Shanghai–Xi'an circuit skips — and a border you can walk across for lunch.

Start with the [China travel checklist](/china-travel/articles/china-travel-checklist-before-you-fly/), and let Zhuhai be the easy part of your trip.
