Setting up WeChat Pay is a short process, but only if you do the steps in the right order. This walkthrough takes one traveller from a phone with no WeChat installed to a completed first payment in China. It covers installation, phone verification, passport identity checks, linking an international card, and then three first-payment flows: you scan a merchant code, a merchant scans your code, and you pay inside a mini program.

Do the setup at home, before you fly, while you have stable internet, your passport and your bank app nearby. Screens and labels change between app versions, so follow the current on-screen prompts rather than hunting for an exact button name.

## Step 1: Install the official WeChat app

Search your app store for WeChat and confirm the developer is Tencent before you install it. Payment lives inside WeChat; there is no separate official "WeChat Pay" app to download, so ignore look-alike tools that promise a shortcut. iPhone users use the App Store, and most other travellers use Google Play. If you already use WeChat for messaging, sign in to that account — you do not create a second profile for payments.

Register with your mobile number. The app asks for a country code and a phone number, then sends a short verification code. This becomes the account you will use for payment later. If you already have an account, simply log in and go straight to the next step. Check the interface language early and set it to one you read comfortably; financial screens are much easier to follow when you are not translating every line.

One more thing to confirm at the start: the app may treat accounts differently by region. The route below is the standard international-card path for visitors. If your app opens on a different first screen, use its own guidance for overseas users and confirm what is available for your account before you continue.

## Step 2: Make sure your phone number can receive codes

Verification is tied to the phone number you registered. Make sure that number can receive international SMS while you are completing setup. A home SIM with roaming enabled is usually the simplest option, and doing this before departure gives you both time and stable connectivity. Keep the phone beside you when you tap through registration and card screens, because the same number may be used more than once.

What you should see is a short code to type into the app, followed by a signed-in account. You may be asked for another code later when you add sensitive information. That is normal for a financial step. The codes are time-limited, so enter them promptly and stay on the same screen until it confirms success. If you plan to use a China eSIM or a local SIM on arrival, sort that out before you begin payment setup — see [the best eSIM and internet options for China travel](/china-travel/articles/best-esim-for-china-travel-internet-access/). Changing your number midway through setup is the one thing that makes this stage harder than it needs to be.

## Step 3: Open the wallet area and find the card-linking screen

WeChat's payment features sit inside the app, not in a separate program. Once you are signed in, find the wallet or payment area — the exact menu name varies by version and language, so look for the section that shows bank cards, balance and payment settings rather than a specific tab label.

On that screen, choose the option to add a bank card. You are creating a payment method tied to your WeChat account; you are not buying anything yet. Read each screen before you type, because the flow will ask for card details and, for most foreign accounts, identity information as well. The order can differ between versions: if the app asks you to confirm your identity first, complete that and then return to the card screen. Treat the step as finished only when the card appears in your list of linked cards, with the last digits visible.

A counter is where all of this pays off: the app on your phone, a linked card and a merchant ready to take payment. You do not need to understand the Chinese labels on a sign to follow the flow in your own app, but you do need the card link and verification completed before you get here.

## Step 4: Confirm your identity with your passport

Identity verification is the part travellers most often underestimate. Have your passport physically in hand. You will need the exact English spelling of your name, the passport number, your nationality and the expiry date. If the app asks for a photo of the details page, capture it cleanly so the corners and the machine-readable lines are visible and nothing is covered by your hand.

Enter everything exactly as printed. Your name on the passport and your name on the bank card should match in spelling and order. If they do not, the app may ask for extra review — confirm in the app what it accepts for your case. Use your real details if a billing address or contact number is requested. Do not invent a local address or borrow someone else's documents; the check is tied to your own identity and your own payment method.

<img src="/china-travel/images/wxsetup-passport-phone.webp" alt="Hand holding a passport beside a smartphone, ready for an in-app identity check" width="1800" height="1193" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

Completing verification is not the same as linking a card. It simply confirms who you are to the payment service, so later card and payment steps do not keep asking for the same documents. Keep the passport nearby until the screen shows the check is complete, then put it away safely. This is an in-app document check, not a visa or entry procedure, and it does not give you a Chinese bank account.

## Step 5: Link your international card

With identity confirmed, go back to the card-linking screen. Enter the card number, expiry date and security code exactly as they appear on the card. If the app asks for a billing address or phone number, use the details your bank holds on file. Keep your bank app accessible while you do this, since your issuer may send its own approval prompt at the same moment. Check that the card you are adding is the one you actually carry with you.

Add one card at a time. Put the card you plan to use most first, and confirm it shows in the linked-card list before you add a backup. When the app offers a choice of default card, pick the one you just linked so you know which card will be charged at the counter. You can add another card later from the same wallet area, but there is no need to do everything on the first pass.

Set a payment password if the app asks for one. This is the short PIN you will use to approve payments in WeChat; it is separate from your bank card PIN. Choose something you will remember and do not write it in your luggage. Some accounts use a six-digit payment password, others confirm with a device unlock — follow what your own screen requires rather than a guide written for a different version.

Two figures are worth knowing before your first payment. Official guidance raised the single-transaction limit for foreign-card mobile payments to USD 5,000 and the annual cumulative limit to USD 50,000. Tencent's 2026 visitor measures include a temporary waiver of the 3% international-card fee for 90 days from the first transaction, on daily spending up to RMB 1,000. These are limits and a promotion, not a promise about your own account — confirm the current figures in the app before relying on them.

## Step 6: Check your setup before you leave home

Before you fly, run a readiness check while you still have time to act on anything. Confirm that WeChat opens and you are signed in, your registered number can receive codes, identity verification shows complete, your card appears in the linked list, and the payment password works. This is a check of states inside the app, not a test purchase.

If the app offers a small test transaction in your home country, use it once to see the confirmation screen. Otherwise, a clean "card linked" state plus completed verification is your signal that setup is done. For a wider check that also covers documents, internet and backup methods, run the [Payment Readiness Checker](/tools/china-payment-readiness-checker/) before departure. On travel day, keep the passport and the card you linked somewhere you can reach without unpacking.

## Step 7: First payment — you scan the merchant's QR code

This is the most common flow in China. At a shop, café or stall, look for a printed QR code stand or a code on the counter. It may be a merchant account code or a personal code; either way the action is the same. Open the scan tool in WeChat and point the camera at the code. Hold the phone steady for a second and let the app recognise it rather than waving the camera around.

<img src="/china-travel/images/wxsetup-qr-scan.webp" alt="Customer aiming a phone at a QR code shown on a counter terminal" width="1800" height="1200" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

What comes next is the part to read slowly. You should see a merchant name, an amount — fixed by the merchant or typed by you — and the payment method selected. Check that the amount matches your purchase and that the method is the international card you linked in Step 5, then approve with your payment password or device unlock. Wait for the success screen before you take the item or leave the table.

For a first payment, choose something inexpensive: a drink, a snack, a convenience-store item. The goal is to see the whole flow once, not to test your card's ceiling. The exact labels on the confirmation screen vary by app version, so match the merchant and the amount, then follow the current on-screen prompt. If the app asks you to choose between cards, pick the one you linked earlier.

## Step 8: Second payment — the merchant scans your code

Many counters work the other way around. Instead of you scanning a code, the cashier asks to scan a payment code on your phone. This is common at busy tills, supermarkets and small food stalls where a QR stand is not practical. It is the same WeChat Pay account and the same linked card; only the direction of the scan changes.

<img src="/china-travel/images/wxsetup-street-stall-payment.webp" alt="Customer using a phone at a Chinese snack stall with QR payment signs on the counter" width="1200" height="1800" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

Open the in-app payment code — the screen that presents your account as a scannable code — and hold the phone steady where the cashier can see it. The code refreshes, so use the one currently on screen rather than a saved image. The cashier scans it, an amount appears, and you approve it. If the terminal shows the amount first, check it before you confirm.

The advantage of this flow is speed: the cashier controls the scanner and you only approve the amount. Confirm that the figure matches your purchase and that the linked card is the selected method. Keep the phone unlocked and the screen bright until the success message appears, then put the phone away before you step aside. Both directions can exist in the same shop, and knowing that they are simply two ways to confirm one payment makes a busy counter much less confusing on your first day.

## Step 9: Third payment — pay inside a mini program

Restaurant QR menus, attraction bookings and similar services run inside WeChat as mini programs. The payment still goes through WeChat Pay, but the checkout happens inside that small app rather than at a staffed counter. You will recognise the pattern once you have seen a table code: scan, browse, order, then pay in the same flow.

<img src="/china-travel/images/wxsetup-contactless-counter.webp" alt="Waiter holding a payment terminal toward a customer's phone at a restaurant table with a QR stand" width="1800" height="1350" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

Scan the code on the table or the poster, load the menu, choose your items and move to the checkout step inside the mini program. When it asks how to pay, choose WeChat Pay and the card you linked in Step 5. Approve the amount with your payment password or device unlock, just as you did at the counter.

What to confirm here is the order total, the merchant name and that you are paying for the right table or order. A mini program can mix ordering, vouchers and payment in one flow, so take an extra second at the final confirmation. After it succeeds, keep the order confirmation on screen until your food or ticket arrives. You have now walked all three paths a visitor normally meets: scanning a merchant code, being scanned at a till, and paying inside a mini program. Each uses the same linked card and the same identity check you completed earlier.

## Your first payment is done

The order of this walkthrough is the point: install, verify the phone number, confirm your identity with a passport, link the card, check the setup, then practise the three payment flows. Each step unlocks the next — verification makes card linking possible, and a linked card makes all three payment flows possible. Once you have completed all three, you can use the same setup wherever you see a payment QR code.
