# China Starter Portal Redesign

Date: 2026-06-04
Project: `/Users/apple/ai-webplan`
Scope: China-facing content experience rooted in travel, but positioned as a broader entry portal for Western users exploring China.

## 1. Product Direction

The site should no longer behave like a standard travel blog or a pure travel-planning assistant.

The new product position is:

`A China starter portal for Western users`

This means the site should help first-time visitors and curious foreign readers:

- understand what China is like
- decide where to go
- prepare for a trip
- use practical tools
- discover culture, food, and everyday life

Travel remains the strongest acquisition path, but it is not the only content boundary anymore.

## 2. Core Problems In The Current Version

The current version is useful, but several product layers are mixed together in a way that weakens the homepage and overall identity.

### 2.1 Homepage role confusion

The homepage currently tries to do too many jobs at once:

- brand introduction
- trust explanation
- article directory
- utility directory
- city recommendation

As a result, it reads like a content hub instead of a guided entry point.

### 2.2 Identity confusion

The site currently sits between these identities without fully committing to one:

- travel guide
- travel planner
- article library
- China portal

This creates friction for users and makes future expansion harder.

### 2.3 Visual hierarchy is too text-first

For a Western audience, especially on a China-related discovery site, the first screen needs stronger visual pull. The current homepage relies too much on reading before desire is created.

### 2.4 Content categories are too flat

Articles, tools, and featured content currently feel similar in weight. The site needs a clearer first-level structure so users know what kind of content they are entering.

### 2.5 The visual system is not yet aligned with the portal goal

The current visual language is clean and functional, but it still feels closer to an information page than a Western-facing China discovery portal.

## 3. Product Goals

The redesign should prioritize these goals:

1. Make the homepage feel like a welcoming "start here" portal instead of a text-heavy content archive.
2. Match Western browsing habits with stronger imagery, clearer sectioning, and lower initial reading load.
3. Keep the experience practical and trustworthy, especially for policy-sensitive content.
4. Create a structure that can grow beyond travel without becoming messy.
5. Preserve room for future monetization without forcing product blocks or sales modules too early.

## 4. Non-Goals

The redesign should not try to do these things yet:

- become a full media magazine
- become a heavy trip-booking product
- become a general China news site
- add ecommerce or service conversion modules now
- solve every page template in phase one

## 5. Primary Audience

Primary audience:

- Western users who are curious about China
- first-time travelers considering a visit
- users preparing for a first trip
- users who want to understand practical daily-life issues in China

Secondary audience:

- users already in China who need tools or practical help
- users exploring China through food, cities, and culture before committing to travel

## 6. Information Architecture

The first-level navigation should shift to this structure:

- `Start Here`
- `Travel`
- `Cities`
- `Life & Culture`
- `Tools`

### 6.1 Why this structure works

This structure allows the site to:

- stay understandable at first glance
- remain travel-led without being travel-only
- support future expansion into broader China discovery content
- prevent the homepage from becoming a generic article list

### 6.2 Section responsibilities

#### Start Here

Purpose:
Give first-time users the shortest path into the site.

Content type:

- beginner journeys
- first steps
- best starting guides
- curated entry cards, not long article grids

Rule:
This section is a guided gateway, not an archive.

#### Travel

Purpose:
Cover practical travel preparation and logistics.

Content type:

- visas
- payments
- apps
- trains
- internet
- planning checklists
- first-trip routes

Rule:
This remains the strongest SEO and trust-building pillar.

#### Cities

Purpose:
Help users choose places, compare destinations, and discover city-level experiences.

Content type:

- city profiles
- destination comparisons
- weekly featured city
- city collections

Rule:
This is one of the most visual sections and should carry strong photography.

#### Life & Culture

Purpose:
Help users understand what China feels like beyond pure logistics.

Content type:

- food
- daily life
- etiquette
- interesting experiences
- practical cultural context

Rule:
This section must stay useful for foreign readers. It should not become a random content bucket for unrelated China topics.

#### Tools

Purpose:
Provide practical utility and reinforce trust.

Content type:

- checklist generator
- transit checker
- city picker
- future utilities

Rule:
Tools should support discovery and planning, not live as isolated side features.

## 7. Homepage Strategy

The homepage should become a distribution layer, not a dense content page.

Its job is:

- create immediate interest
- orient the user quickly
- route users into the correct content type
- establish trust without over-explaining

### 7.1 Homepage order

Recommended homepage module order:

1. Hero
2. Start Here
3. Cities
4. Life & Culture
5. Tools
6. Featured City or Weekly Recommendation

### 7.2 Hero

Role:
Create desire and set the site's identity in one glance.

Requirements:

- strong real photography
- short headline
- short supporting line
- two clear calls to action

Suggested CTA logic:

- primary: enter a guided path
- secondary: explore destinations or cities

Rule:
The hero should not attempt to explain the whole site.

### 7.3 Start Here

Role:
This is the most important module directly below the hero.

Suggested presentation:

- 3 large entry cards
- beginner-friendly labels
- each card leads into a clear journey

Suggested card themes:

- planning a trip
- choosing a city
- understanding modern China

Rule:
This module should feel like a guided beginning, not an article shelf.

### 7.4 Cities

Role:
Provide visual exploration and keep users browsing.

Suggested presentation:

- photo-forward city cards
- short value proposition per city
- a "see all cities" path

Rule:
Cities should not be reduced to generic tourist attraction lists. Each city needs a clear point of view such as "best for food", "best first city", "best for slower travel", or similar.

### 7.5 Life & Culture

Role:
Expand the site beyond pure travel logistics and give the portal more depth.

Suggested presentation:

- image-led topic cards
- fewer but better-curated themes
- strong emphasis on relevance to foreign readers

### 7.6 Tools

Role:
Show usefulness without overpowering the emotional or exploratory parts of the homepage.

Suggested presentation:

- concise tool cards
- strong utility language
- linked from related content

Rule:
Tools should appear as part of the portal system, not as a separate product island.

### 7.7 Featured City or Weekly Recommendation

Role:
Provide a highlight area where the site can feel more vivid and special.

Use cases:

- weekly city spotlight
- seasonal destination feature
- travel moment or city collection

Rule:
This block can carry more distinct Chinese visual accents than the rest of the site.

## 8. Visual Direction

The chosen design direction is:

- base system: clean map-and-utility
- accent system: modern East-meets-global only in selective highlight areas

### 8.1 Base visual system

Use a calm, Western-friendly foundation:

- cloud white
- pale blue-gray
- muted teal or green-blue
- soft slate neutrals

This should make the site feel:

- organized
- readable
- trustworthy
- easy to browse

### 8.2 Accent system

Use restrained China-coded accents only in select areas:

- featured city
- weekly recommendation
- key badges
- limited editorial emphasis

Accent palette can include:

- deep brick red
- tea gold
- parchment-toned warm neutrals

### 8.3 What to avoid

- using red as a dominant site-wide base color
- beige-heavy pages with weak contrast
- overly generic blue SaaS styling with no cultural identity

## 9. Content Hierarchy Rules

The site should adopt these hierarchy rules:

- homepage is for routing, not deep reading
- first-level categories are for orientation
- second-level pages are for expansion
- articles support categories, not replace them
- tools should reinforce article pathways

This means the homepage should reduce the number of raw article links visible at once and increase the number of visual category entrances.

## 10. Image Strategy

The image system needs a more disciplined policy.

### 10.1 Priority order

1. real, traceable, usable photography
2. high-confidence stock or official visuals
3. AI-generated visuals for explanatory content only

### 10.2 Recommended use by content type

Use real photography for:

- homepage hero
- cities
- culture
- destination collections
- weekly features

Use generated or designed visuals for:

- tools
- explainers
- checklists
- information graphics
- fallback covers where real photography is not available

### 10.3 Social-platform sourcing rule

Platforms such as Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Instagram, or TikTok should be treated as:

- trend radar
- composition inspiration
- topic discovery sources

They should not be treated as a default production image library because of:

- copyright risk
- platform terms risk
- watermark and attribution problems
- future monetization and platform review risk

### 10.4 Image governance

Important images should eventually be tracked with:

- page usage
- source origin
- commercial-use status
- attribution requirement
- whether it is source material or only inspiration

## 11. Future Monetization Readiness

The product should stay monetization-ready without making monetization visible too early.

This means:

- preserve room for future featured placements
- keep category structure broad enough for future products or services
- build trust and return value first

Monetization should not affect current navigation or homepage priorities yet.

## 12. Phase One Implementation Scope

Phase one should focus on structural correction, not full site completion.

Recommended phase one scope:

1. Reframe the homepage around the new portal identity.
2. Replace flat article-heavy presentation with clear first-level category modules.
3. Introduce the new first-level section structure in navigation and homepage routing.
4. Define and apply a stronger visual system.
5. Improve the image strategy for hero, category cards, city cards, and feature sections.

## 13. Risks And Guardrails

### Risk 1: The site becomes too broad too quickly

Guardrail:
Travel remains the strongest operating pillar even if the identity broadens.

### Risk 2: Life & Culture becomes a junk drawer

Guardrail:
Every topic must answer "why does this matter to a foreign reader learning about China?"

### Risk 3: The homepage becomes beautiful but unclear

Guardrail:
Routing clarity is more important than decoration.

### Risk 4: Tools become detached from the rest of the site

Guardrail:
Every tool should connect back into categories, articles, or city pages.

### Risk 5: The visual system overuses Chinese motifs

Guardrail:
China identity should be a controlled accent, not a full-page costume.

## 14. Verification Criteria

The redesign is successful if:

- a first-time foreign user can understand the site's purpose within one screen
- the homepage feels more image-led and less text-heavy
- top-level categories are clear without reading long explanations
- cities and culture feel distinct from logistics
- tools feel useful but not dominant
- the site is easier to expand without homepage clutter

## 15. Recommended Next Step

After design approval, the next step should be an implementation plan that breaks the redesign into staged execution:

- homepage restructuring
- navigation and taxonomy updates
- new landing pages for first-level sections
- visual system refresh
- selective image refit
